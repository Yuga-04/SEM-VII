## Explain Different Types of Prototypes and the Concept of Fidelity

### Introduction

A **prototype** is an early, simplified version of a product, service, system, or solution created to explore an idea and obtain feedback before developing the final solution. Prototypes can differ in their **level of detail, functionality, and similarity to the final product**. Design thinking unit 4 notes

---

## 1. Types of Prototypes

The document describes prototypes based on their form and level of development.

### A. Low-Fidelity Prototypes

A **low-fidelity prototype** is a simple and inexpensive representation of an idea.

**Examples:**
- Paper sketches
- Storyboards
- Hand-drawn interfaces
- Wireframes
- Role-playing
- Card-based models Design thinking unit 4 notes

**Advantages:**
- Quick to create
- Low cost
- Easy to modify
- Suitable for early-stage ideas Design thinking unit 4 notes

**Example:**  
A designer can draw the screens of a mobile banking application on paper before developing the actual application.

---

### B. Medium-Fidelity Prototypes

A **medium-fidelity prototype** contains more structure and interaction than a low-fidelity prototype but is not completely functional.

**Examples:**
- Digital wireframes
- Clickable screen designs
- Basic interactive mock-ups Design thinking unit 4 notes

It is useful when the designer wants to test the **structure and basic interaction** of a proposed solution.

---

### C. High-Fidelity Prototypes

A **high-fidelity prototype** closely resembles the final product in terms of appearance, interaction, and functionality.

**Examples:**
- Fully interactive application prototype
- Functional hardware model
- Detailed product model Design thinking unit 4 notes

**Advantages:**
- Provides a realistic user experience
- Useful for usability testing
- Helps stakeholders visualize the final product Design thinking unit 4 notes

---

### D. Service Prototypes

Service prototypes represent how a **service is delivered and experienced by users**.

**Examples:**
- Customer journey simulations
- Role-playing customer interactions
- Service blueprints
- Simulated service environments Design thinking unit 4 notes

---

# 2. Concept of Fidelity

**Fidelity** refers to **how closely a prototype represents the final product** in terms of:

- Appearance
- Functionality
- Interaction
- Level of detail Design thinking unit 4 notes

In simple words:

> **Fidelity = How similar the prototype is to the final product.**

### Fidelity Levels

| Fidelity | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | Simple, basic representation | Paper sketch, storyboard |
| **Medium** | More structure and interaction | Digital wireframe, clickable mock-up |
| **High** | Closely resembles final product | Interactive app, functional hardware |

### Comparison

**Low Fidelity**
→ Simple  
→ Cheap  
→ Fast  
→ Easy to modify  
→ Used for early ideas

**Medium Fidelity**
→ More structure  
→ Basic interaction  
→ Used to test screen flow and interaction

**High Fidelity**
→ Detailed  
→ Realistic interaction  
→ More time/resources required  
→ Used for detailed testing

---

## Conclusion

Different types of prototypes are selected according to the **purpose and stage of development**. Low-fidelity prototypes are useful for exploring early ideas, medium-fidelity prototypes help test structure and interaction, and high-fidelity prototypes provide a realistic representation of the final product. **Fidelity determines how closely the prototype represents the final solution**, helping designers choose the appropriate prototype for testing and development.
