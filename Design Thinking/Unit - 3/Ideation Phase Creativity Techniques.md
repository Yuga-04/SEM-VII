# Brainstorming – 13 Marks

### Definition

**Brainstorming** is a structured group technique used to generate a large number of ideas for a clearly defined problem. During the initial stage, ideas are generated **without criticism or immediate judgment**. 

### Objectives

* Generate many possible ideas.
* Encourage creative thinking.
* Get different perspectives.
* Encourage teamwork.
* Avoid selecting a solution too early. 

### Principles

1. **Defer judgment** – Do not criticize ideas initially.
2. **Encourage quantity** – Generate as many ideas as possible.
3. **Accept unusual ideas** – Welcome creative and unconventional ideas.
4. **Build on ideas** – Improve or combine ideas given by others.
5. **Keep an open mind** – Consider different perspectives. 

### Process

**1. Prepare:**
Clearly define the problem, explain the objective, select participants, set a time limit, and explain the rules.

**2. Generate ideas:**
Participants freely give ideas. The facilitator records them and prevents criticism.

**3. Build on ideas:**
Ideas can be combined, expanded, simplified, reversed, or adapted.

**4. Organize and evaluate:**
After brainstorming, similar ideas are grouped and evaluated using criteria such as **feasibility, desirability, cost, viability, and user impact**. 

### Role of Facilitator

The facilitator:

* Encourages everyone to participate.
* Prevents dominant members from controlling the session.
* Manages time.
* Records ideas.
* Prevents criticism during idea generation. 

### Example

**Problem:** Long waiting time in a college canteen.

Brainstorming ideas may include:

* Mobile food ordering
* Digital menu
* Online payment
* Token system
* Order notifications
* Self-service kiosks

The ideas are later evaluated, and a promising solution such as **mobile pre-ordering with notifications** can be selected. 

### Advantages

* Generates many alternatives quickly.
* Uses multiple perspectives.
* Encourages collaboration.
* Promotes creative thinking.
* Provides ideas for later evaluation. 

### Limitations

* Dominant participants may control the discussion.
* Social pressure may reduce participation.
* Familiar ideas may be repeated.
* Groupthink may occur. 

### Conclusion

Brainstorming helps a team **generate many creative ideas without immediate criticism**. These ideas are later organized, evaluated, selected, and refined for further development.

# Mind Mapping – 13 Marks

### Definition

**Mind Mapping** is a visual ideation technique in which a **central topic or problem** is expanded through **branches and sub-branches** of related ideas. It helps organize thoughts and identify relationships between ideas. 

### Objectives

* Organize ideas visually.
* Identify relationships between concepts.
* Generate additional ideas.
* Explore a problem from different directions.
* Handle complex problems with many factors. 

### Structure of a Mind Map

A mind map consists of:

1. **Central Topic** – Main problem or theme.
2. **Main Branches** – Major areas related to the problem.
3. **Sub-branches** – Specific ideas or solutions.
4. **Connections** – Relationships between related ideas. 

### Process

**1. Start with the central problem**
Write the main problem at the center.

**2. Identify main branches**
Find the major areas related to the problem.

**3. Add sub-branches**
Expand each branch with specific ideas.

**4. Find connections**
Look for relationships, patterns, gaps, and new opportunities. 

### Example – College Transportation

**Central Topic:** Improve College Transportation

```text
             COLLEGE TRANSPORTATION
                      |
        ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
        ↓             ↓             ↓
     Safety       Convenience      Cost
        |             |             |
     CCTV        Mobile App      Route
     GPS         Online Booking   Optimization
     Emergency   Live Location    Fuel Monitoring
     Button
```

Another branch can be **Communication → SMS Alerts → Mobile Notifications**. This example is given in your notes. 

### Advantages

* Easy to understand visually.
* Encourages free association.
* Shows relationships between ideas.
* Generates additional ideas.
* Organizes complex information.
* Useful for both individual and group ideation. 

### Limitation

A mind map can become **crowded and confusing** if too many unrelated ideas are added. Therefore, ideas should remain connected to the main design challenge. 

### Conclusion

Mind Mapping helps designers **visually explore, organize, connect, and expand ideas** around a central problem. It is especially useful when a problem has many related factors.

# Storyboarding – 13 Marks

### Definition

**Storyboarding** is a visual technique used to represent a user's experience or a proposed solution through a **sequence of scenes or frames**. It shows how an experience or interaction unfolds over time. 

### Purpose of Storyboarding

* Visualize the **user experience**.
* Show the sequence of actions and interactions.
* Identify missing steps or problems.
* Communicate a solution clearly.
* Understand how an idea changes the user's experience. 

### Elements of a Storyboard

A storyboard commonly includes:

1. **User**
2. **Setting**
3. **Starting situation**
4. **Actions**
5. **Interactions**
6. **Emotional responses**
7. **Final outcome** 

### Steps in Storyboarding

**1. Identify the user**
Determine who is experiencing the problem.

**2. Identify the user's problem**
Understand the difficulty faced by the user.

**3. Show the current situation**
Show what happens before the proposed solution.

**4. Introduce the solution**
Show the proposed idea or solution.

**5. Show user interaction**
Show how the user interacts with the solution.

**6. Present the improved outcome**
Show the final result after using the solution. 

### Example – Online Hospital Appointment

```text id="r4g7sk"
Patient needs doctor
        ↓
Opens hospital application
        ↓
Selects doctor & time
        ↓
Appointment confirmed
        ↓
Receives reminder
        ↓
Visits hospital at scheduled time
```

This storyboard shows the user's experience **step-by-step**, from the initial problem to the improved outcome. 

### Why is Storyboarding Useful in Ideation?

A solution may sound good when explained in words, but visualizing it as a sequence helps the team understand:

* **Timing**
* **Context**
* **User interactions**
* **Consequences**
* **Missing steps**

Thus, storyboarding helps designers understand whether a proposed idea actually improves the user's experience. 

### Advantages

* Makes abstract ideas concrete.
* Encourages user-centred thinking.
* Reveals gaps in the proposed experience.
* Improves communication.
* Makes ideas easier to understand.
* Supports user-centered design. 

### Limitation

A storyboard **does not prove technical feasibility or user acceptance**. Further **prototyping and testing** are required. 

### Conclusion

Storyboarding helps designers **visualize and communicate a user's experience step-by-step**. It allows the team to identify problems, understand interactions, and improve solution ideas before moving to prototyping.

# SCAMPER – 13 Marks

### Definition

**SCAMPER** is a creativity technique used to **modify existing products, services, or ideas to generate new solutions**. It provides seven different ways to change or improve an existing idea. 

### SCAMPER stands for:

| Letter | Component           |
| ------ | ------------------- |
| **S**  | Substitute          |
| **C**  | Combine             |
| **A**  | Adapt               |
| **M**  | Modify              |
| **P**  | Put to another use  |
| **E**  | Eliminate           |
| **R**  | Reverse / Rearrange |



---

## 1. S – Substitute

**Meaning:** Replace one part, material, method, or component with another.

**Question:**

> *What can be substituted?*

**Example:** Replace a paper menu with a **digital menu**.

---

## 2. C – Combine

**Meaning:** Combine two or more existing ideas, features, or functions.

**Question:**

> *What can be combined?*

**Example:** Combine **food ordering + online payment** into one system.

---

## 3. A – Adapt

**Meaning:** Adapt an existing idea or solution to a new situation or purpose.

**Question:**

> *What can be adapted?*

**Example:** Adapt **online order tracking** for tracking college canteen orders.

---

## 4. M – Modify

**Meaning:** Change, improve, enlarge, reduce, or alter an existing idea.

**Question:**

> *What can be modified or improved?*

**Example:** Modify the canteen process to make ordering faster.

---

## 5. P – Put to Another Use

**Meaning:** Use an existing product, idea, or system for a different purpose.

**Question:**

> *Can it be used for another purpose?*

**Example:** Use a **college ID card** not only for identification but also for making canteen payments.

---

## 6. E – Eliminate

**Meaning:** Remove unnecessary parts, steps, or features.

**Question:**

> *What can be eliminated?*

**Example:** Eliminate unnecessary **waiting in the canteen queue** by introducing pre-ordering.

---

## 7. R – Reverse / Rearrange

**Meaning:** Reverse or change the order of an existing process.

**Question:**

> *Can the process be reversed or rearranged?*

**Example:**

Normal:

**Arrive → Order → Pay → Wait → Collect**

Rearranged:

**Order → Pay → Food prepared → Arrive → Collect**

---

# Example of SCAMPER

### Problem:

**College canteen has long waiting times.**

SCAMPER can be applied as:

* **S – Substitute:** Paper menu → Digital menu
* **C – Combine:** Ordering + Payment
* **A – Adapt:** Online shopping tracking → Food-order tracking
* **M – Modify:** Improve counter arrangement
* **P – Put to another use:** College ID → Payment
* **E – Eliminate:** Unnecessary waiting
* **R – Rearrange:** Order before arriving

Thus, SCAMPER helps the team **look at an existing system in seven different ways and develop new solution possibilities**.

---

## Advantages

* Helps generate new ideas from existing ideas.
* Encourages creative thinking.
* Helps overcome **idea fixation**.
* Provides different directions for improving a solution.
* Supports innovation.

Your document specifically identifies SCAMPER as a technique that can help overcome **idea fixation**. 

---

## Conclusion

SCAMPER is a simple and systematic creativity technique for **improving existing products, services, or ideas**. By using **Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, and Reverse/Rearrange**, designers can discover alternative and innovative solutions.

# Lateral Thinking – 13 Marks

### Definition

**Lateral Thinking** is a creative problem-solving approach that encourages people to **move away from conventional thinking patterns**. It involves looking at a problem from **unusual perspectives** and considering unexpected alternatives. 

### Simple Meaning

> **Lateral Thinking = Think differently from the usual way.**

It helps designers find innovative solutions that may not come from traditional logical thinking.

---

## Objectives of Lateral Thinking

* To overcome **conventional thinking**.
* To look at a problem from different perspectives.
* To question existing assumptions.
* To find unexpected alternatives.
* To generate innovative solutions. 

---

## How Lateral Thinking Works

The basic process is:

**Problem → Question the usual approach → Look from a different perspective → Generate unusual alternatives → Find a new solution**

Instead of accepting the first or obvious solution, the designer asks:

> **"Is there another way to look at this problem?"**

---

## Example

### Problem:

**Students spend a lot of time waiting in a college canteen.**

### Conventional thinking:

> "How can we make the queue faster?"

Possible answer:

> Add another counter.

### Lateral thinking:

Instead ask:

> **"How can we eliminate the need for students to wait in a queue?"**

This can lead to ideas such as:

* Mobile pre-ordering
* Online payment
* Digital ordering
* Order notifications

So, lateral thinking changes the **way the problem is viewed**, rather than simply improving the existing method.

---

## Important Characteristics

### 1. Unconventional Thinking

Moves away from normal or familiar solutions.

### 2. Different Perspectives

Looks at the problem from different viewpoints.

### 3. Unexpected Alternatives

Considers solutions that may not be obvious.

### 4. Challenges Conventional Patterns

Questions the normal way of doing something.

### 5. Encourages Innovation

Can produce solutions that traditional thinking may not discover. 

---

## Lateral Thinking and Idea Fixation

**Idea fixation** occurs when people become attached to an existing idea and stop exploring alternatives. 

Lateral thinking helps overcome this by encouraging designers to:

> **Break away from the existing solution and explore different possibilities.**

Your document specifically mentions **SCAMPER and lateral thinking** as techniques for overcoming idea fixation. 

---

## Advantages

* Helps overcome conventional thinking.
* Produces alternative solutions.
* Encourages creativity.
* Helps overcome idea fixation.
* Encourages innovative thinking.
* Provides different perspectives on a problem.

---

## Conclusion

Lateral Thinking is an important creativity technique in the **Ideate phase of Design Thinking**. It encourages designers to **move beyond conventional approaches, question existing assumptions, consider unusual perspectives, and generate unexpected solutions**.
