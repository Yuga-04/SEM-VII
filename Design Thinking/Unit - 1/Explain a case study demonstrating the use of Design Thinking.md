# Case Study Demonstrating the Use of Design Thinking – 13 Marks

## Introduction

Design Thinking is a **human-centered approach to problem-solving**. It helps understand the real problems faced by users and develop suitable solutions through **Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Test**. A good example is the **hospital patient experience case study** given in the document. 

## Case Study: Improving Hospital Patient Experience

### Problem Situation

Patients visiting the hospital were experiencing **long waiting times and poor communication** with hospital staff. These problems affected their overall hospital experience and satisfaction.

The objective was to understand the difficulties faced by patients and develop a better system for managing appointments and providing information.

---

## 1. Empathize

The first step was to understand the experiences of the actual users.

The team **interviewed patients and hospital staff** to understand:

* Problems faced by patients.
* Difficulties during their hospital visit.
* Issues related to waiting.
* Problems in communication between patients and staff.

This helped the team understand the problem from the **users' point of view** rather than making assumptions. 

---

## 2. Define

After collecting information from patients and staff, the team analyzed the problems and identified the major issues.

The main problems were:

* **Long waiting times**
* **Poor communication**

These problems were clearly defined so that the team could focus on developing solutions that would directly improve the patient experience. 

---

## 3. Ideate

In this stage, the team generated different ideas to solve the identified problems.

Some of the important ideas were:

* **Digital appointment scheduling system**
* **Patient information kiosks**

A digital appointment system could help organize appointments and reduce unnecessary waiting. Information kiosks could provide patients with useful information and improve communication.

The purpose of this stage was to explore possible solutions before selecting an appropriate approach. 

---

## 4. Prototype

After generating ideas, the team developed a **basic digital appointment scheduling system** as a prototype.

The prototype provided an early version of the proposed solution. Instead of implementing a complete system immediately, the team could first check whether the basic system was useful and suitable for patients.

This also allowed the team to identify possible improvements before full implementation. 

---

## 5. Test

The prototype was then tested by **patients**.

Patients used the system and provided their **feedback**. The team used this feedback to understand whether the system solved the identified problems and what further improvements were required.

Testing is important because it allows the solution to be refined based on actual user experience. 

---

## Results

The use of Design Thinking produced several positive results:

1. **Reduced waiting times** – The appointment system helped improve the management of appointments.
2. **Improved patient satisfaction** – Patients received a better overall experience.
3. **Better communication** – Communication between hospital staff and patients improved. 

---

## Design Thinking Flow

**Hospital Patient Problem**
↓
**Empathize** – Interview patients and staff
↓
**Define** – Long waiting times + poor communication
↓
**Ideate** – Digital appointments + information kiosks
↓
**Prototype** – Basic appointment scheduling system
↓
**Test** – Patients test and provide feedback
↓
**Result** – Reduced waiting + better satisfaction + improved communication

## Conclusion

This case study shows how Design Thinking can be used to solve a **real-world service problem**. By understanding patients, clearly defining their problems, generating suitable ideas, creating a prototype and testing it with users, the hospital was able to improve the **patient experience, waiting time and communication**. It demonstrates that Design Thinking focuses on creating solutions based on **actual user needs and feedback**.
