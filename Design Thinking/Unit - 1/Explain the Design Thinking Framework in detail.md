# Design Thinking Framework – 13 Marks

**Design Thinking** is a human-centered approach to problem-solving and innovation. It focuses on understanding users, identifying their actual problems, generating creative solutions, developing prototypes, and improving them through testing and feedback. 

The **Design Thinking Framework** consists of five stages:

### **Empathise → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test**

---

## 1. Empathise

Empathise is the **first stage** of Design Thinking. It focuses on understanding the **needs, feelings, behavior, experiences and difficulties of users**.

The designer interacts with users and studies their real situations instead of making assumptions.

**Methods used:**

* Observation
* Interviews
* Conversation and interaction
* User research

**Purpose:** To understand the user's actual needs and identify problems that they may not directly express. 

---

## 2. Define

The Define stage converts the information collected during the Empathise stage into a **clear and focused problem**.

The team analyzes the collected information, identifies patterns and prepares a **problem statement**.

A good problem definition should be:

* Clear
* Specific
* User-centered
* Meaningful
* Actionable

**Example:** Instead of saying *“The canteen is crowded,”* the actual problem may be that students experience long waiting times because ordering and payment take too long. 

**Output:** A clear and focused problem statement.

---

## 3. Ideate

Ideate is the stage of **generating many possible solutions** to the defined problem.

The main objective is to explore different possibilities rather than immediately selecting one solution.

**Important principles:**

* Generate multiple alternatives.
* Encourage creativity.
* Build on others' ideas.
* Avoid early criticism.
* Keep the user problem in focus.

**Techniques used:**

* Brainstorming
* Mind Mapping
* SCAMPER
* Storyboarding

**Example:** For a canteen problem, ideas may include online pre-ordering, QR ordering, token systems, separate counters and faster payment. 

---

## 4. Prototype

A **prototype** is an early representation or model of a proposed solution. It makes an idea **tangible and testable**.

A prototype does not need to be the final product. It is created mainly to understand the idea, identify problems and learn from users.

**Examples:**

* Paper sketches
* Wireframes
* Interface mock-ups
* Physical models
* Digital mock-ups

**Benefits:**

* Makes ideas easier to understand.
* Identifies problems early.
* Allows users to give feedback.
* Helps make changes at low cost. 

---

## 5. Test

The Test stage involves **evaluating the prototype with users** and collecting their feedback.

The team observes how users interact with the prototype and identifies its strengths, weaknesses and unexpected problems.

**Activities:**

* User trials
* Observation
* Feedback collection
* Identifying improvements

Testing helps determine whether the solution actually satisfies the intended user need. 

---

## Iterative Nature of Design Thinking

Design Thinking is **not a rigid or completely linear process**. It is an **iterative cycle**.

If testing shows that the solution is not suitable, the team can return to the **Empathise or Define stage**, generate new ideas, create another prototype and test again.

**Prototype → Test → Feedback → Revision → Retest** 

---

## Example – College Canteen

Suppose students face **long waiting times in the college canteen**:

* **Empathise:** Observe the queues and talk to students.
* **Define:** Identify the need for faster and convenient ordering.
* **Ideate:** Generate ideas such as QR ordering, pre-ordering and token systems.
* **Prototype:** Create a simple ordering interface or service flow.
* **Test:** Allow students to use it and collect feedback.
* **Iteration:** Improve the process and test it again. 

---

## Conclusion

The Design Thinking Framework provides a **human-centered, creative and iterative approach** to solving problems. The five stages—**Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Test**—help designers understand users, find the right problem, explore different solutions and continuously improve the final solution.

### **Easy way to remember:**

**E → D → I → P → T**

**Empathise** – Understand users
**Define** – Identify the problem
**Ideate** – Generate ideas
**Prototype** – Create a model
**Test** – Get feedback and improve
