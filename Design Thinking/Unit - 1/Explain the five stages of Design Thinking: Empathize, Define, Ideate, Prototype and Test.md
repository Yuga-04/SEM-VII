# Five Stages of Design Thinking – 13 Marks

**Design Thinking** is a human-centered approach to problem-solving and innovation. It consists of five major stages: **Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Test**. These stages help in understanding users, identifying problems, developing solutions and continuously improving them. 

### **Framework**

**Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test**

---

## 1. Empathize

**Empathize** is the first stage. It focuses on understanding the **needs, feelings, behavior, experiences and difficulties of users**.

### Methods:

* Observation
* Interviews
* Conversation and interaction
* User research

The main purpose is to understand the user's situation and reduce assumptions about their needs. 

**Output:** User information and insights.

---

## 2. Define

The **Define** stage converts the information collected during Empathize into a **clear and focused problem statement**.

The team analyzes the information, identifies patterns and determines the **actual user problem**.

A good problem definition should be:

* Clear
* Specific
* User-centered
* Meaningful
* Actionable

**Output:** A clear problem statement. 

---

## 3. Ideate

**Ideate** means generating **multiple creative ideas and possible solutions** for the defined problem.

The aim is to explore different alternatives instead of immediately selecting the first idea.

### Techniques:

* Brainstorming
* Mind Mapping
* SCAMPER
* Storyboarding

**Output:** A variety of possible solutions. 

---

## 4. Prototype

A **prototype** is an early representation or model of a proposed solution. It makes an idea **tangible and testable**.

### Examples:

* Paper sketches
* Wireframes
* Interface mock-ups
* Physical models
* Digital mock-ups

### Benefits:

* Identifies problems early.
* Makes ideas easier to understand.
* Allows users to react to the solution.
* Supports inexpensive changes and experimentation. 

**Output:** A testable model of the solution.

---

## 5. Test

The **Test** stage involves evaluating the prototype with users and collecting their **feedback and reactions**.

### Activities:

* User trials
* Observation
* Feedback collection
* Identifying strengths and weaknesses
* Finding unexpected problems

Testing helps determine whether the solution actually satisfies the user's needs. 

**Output:** Feedback that helps improve the solution.

---

## Iterative Nature

The five stages should **not be considered a rigid linear process**. Design Thinking is an **iterative cycle**. If testing reveals a new problem, the team can return to the Empathize or Define stage and improve the solution.

**Prototype → Test → Feedback → Revision → Retest** 

### Conclusion

Thus, the five stages of Design Thinking help transform a **user problem into an effective solution**:

**Empathize** – Understand users
**Define** – Identify the actual problem
**Ideate** – Generate ideas
**Prototype** – Create a model
**Test** – Get feedback and improve
