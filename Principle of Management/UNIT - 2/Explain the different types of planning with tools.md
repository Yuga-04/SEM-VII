# Types of Planning with Tools – 13 Mark Answer

## 1. Introduction

Planning is the process of deciding **in advance what to do, how to do it and when to do it** to achieve organizational objectives. The PDF classifies plans mainly into **Strategic, Tactical, Operational and Contingency plans**. 

### Types of Planning

**Planning → Strategic → Tactical → Operational → Contingency**

---

## 2. Strategic Planning

Strategic planning is concerned with the **long-term objectives of the entire organization**. It is mainly prepared by top-level management and provides a framework for lower-level planning. 

### Tools of Strategic Planning

* **Mission** – states the basic purpose of the organization.
* **Objectives/Goals** – specify measurable results to be achieved.
* **Strategies** – determine long-term actions and resource allocation. 

**Example:** A company plans to become a leading electric vehicle manufacturer within five years.

---

## 3. Tactical Planning

Tactical planning converts broad strategic plans into **specific actions**. It has a shorter time period and narrower scope than strategic planning and is generally prepared by middle-level managers. 

### Tool

* **Tactical action plans** – specify **what should be done, how it should be done and who is responsible**.

**Example:** To support an EV strategy, the marketing department plans to increase sales through online campaigns and dealership expansion.

---

## 4. Operational Planning

Operational planning deals with **day-to-day activities** and specific, measurable results. It is normally prepared by supervisors and team leaders. 

Operational plans are of two types:

### a) Single-Use Plans

Used for activities that **do not repeat**.

**Tools:**

* **Programme** – ordered list of events for completing a project.
* **Budget** – estimates income and expenditure for a specific activity. 

**Example:** A company prepares a one-time programme and budget for launching a new product.

### b) Standing Plans

Used repeatedly for routine activities.

**Tools:**

* **Policy** – broad guideline for decision-making.
* **Procedure** – step-by-step method for performing an activity.
* **Rule** – specifies what employees can and cannot do. 

**Example:** A company's employee recruitment policy and purchase procedure are standing plans.

---

## 5. Contingency Planning

Contingency planning means preparing **alternative courses of action** when the original plan fails or circumstances change. It provides flexibility and helps the organization handle unexpected situations. 

**Example:** If a company's main supplier suddenly stops supplying raw materials, it can switch to an alternative supplier.

---

## 6. Summary

| Type            | Main Purpose                 | Important Tools                            |
| --------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Strategic**   | Long-term direction          | Mission, Objectives, Strategies            |
| **Tactical**    | Convert strategy into action | Action plans                               |
| **Operational** | Day-to-day activities        | Programme, Budget, Policy, Procedure, Rule |
| **Contingency** | Handle unexpected situations | Alternative plans                          |

## Conclusion

Thus, **strategic plans provide direction, tactical plans convert strategy into actions, operational plans manage daily activities, and contingency plans handle unexpected situations**. Together, they help an organization achieve its objectives effectively.
