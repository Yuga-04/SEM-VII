# Strategic Management – 13 Mark Answer

## 1. Meaning of Strategic Management

**Strategic management** is the process of determining the **long-term objectives** of an organization and developing suitable strategies, allocating resources and taking actions to achieve those objectives.

According to **Koontz and O'Donnell**, strategy denotes a **general programme of action and deployment of emphasis and resources to attain comprehensive objectives**. Strategies are formulated considering the competitive environment of the business. 

---

## 2. Strategic Management Process

The strategic management/strategic formulation process consists of the following steps:

### 1. Input to the Organization

The organization considers inputs such as **people, capital, management skills, technical skills** and the goals of stakeholders such as employees, customers, suppliers, shareholders, government and the community. 

**Example:** A company considers its financial resources, skilled employees and customer requirements before developing a strategy.

### 2. Industry Analysis

The external environment and attractiveness of the industry are analyzed. The organization considers **competition, new entrants, substitute products and the bargaining power of suppliers and customers**. 

**Example:** A smartphone company studies competitors such as Samsung and Apple before entering a new market.

### 3. Enterprise Profile

The organization identifies its **present position, strengths, weaknesses, resources and capabilities**. This helps determine what the organization can achieve.

### 4. Orientation, Values and Vision of Executives

The values, attitudes, preferences and **vision of top executives** influence the direction of strategy. Their attitude towards risk also affects strategic decisions. 

### 5. Mission, Objectives and Strategic Intent

The organization defines its **mission or purpose**, major objectives and strategic intent. Mission answers the question **“What is our business?”**, while objectives specify the results to be achieved. 

**Example:** A company may set an objective of becoming a market leader within five years.

### 6. Analysis of External Environment

The present and future external environment is studied to identify **opportunities and threats**. 

### 7. Analysis of Internal Environment

Internal resources and capabilities are evaluated to identify **strengths and weaknesses** in areas such as production, marketing, finance, human resources and technology. 

### 8. Development of Alternative Strategies

Based on internal and external analysis, different strategies are developed. These may include **specialization, diversification, joint ventures and strategic alliances**. 

### 9. Evaluation and Choice of Strategy

Alternative strategies are evaluated based on **risk, profitability and timing**, and the most suitable strategy is selected. 

### 10. Implementation and Control

The selected strategy is implemented through **organization structure, staffing, leadership and control**. Performance is monitored against the planned objectives. 

### 11. Consistency Testing and Contingency Planning

Finally, the strategy is tested for **consistency** and contingency plans are prepared to deal with unexpected changes or failure of the original strategy. 

---

## 3. Simple Flow

**Inputs → Industry Analysis → Enterprise Profile → Vision & Values → Mission & Objectives → External/Internal Analysis → Alternative Strategies → Evaluation & Choice → Implementation & Control → Consistency & Contingency Planning**

## Conclusion

Strategic management provides a **long-term direction** to an organization. It helps management understand its environment, identify opportunities and threats, utilize its strengths, overcome weaknesses and select suitable strategies for achieving organizational objectives.
