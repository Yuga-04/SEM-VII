# Decision-Making Process – 13 Mark Answer

## 1. Introduction

**Decision-making** is the process of selecting the **best alternative** from two or more possible alternatives to achieve a specific objective.

The decision-making process consists of **seven steps**: 

**Specific Objective → Problem Identification → Search for Alternatives → Evaluation → Choice → Action → Results**

---

## 2. Steps in Decision-Making Process

### 1. Specific Objective

The decision-making process starts with identifying the **objective to be achieved** and determining whether a decision is required. 

**Example:** A company wants to increase its sales by 20%.

### 2. Problem Identification

The actual problem is identified clearly. Managers diagnose the problem by finding the **gap between the present situation and the desired situation** and analyzing its causes. 

**Example:** Sales are falling because customers prefer competitors' products.

### 3. Search for Alternatives

Different possible solutions are identified. Managers can use **past experience, practices followed by others and creative techniques** to find alternatives. 

**Example:** Improve product quality, reduce price, increase advertising or introduce a new product.

### 4. Evaluation of Alternatives

Each alternative is evaluated according to its **cost, benefits, limitations and contribution towards the objective**. Unsuitable alternatives are eliminated. 

### 5. Choice of Alternative

The expected results of different alternatives are compared and the **best alternative** is selected. 

**Example:** The company chooses to improve product quality because it is expected to increase customer satisfaction and sales.

### 6. Action

The selected alternative is **put into action**. The decision becomes meaningful only when it is properly implemented. 

### 7. Results

The results obtained after implementation are compared with the **original objectives**. This shows whether the decision and its implementation were successful. 

---

## 3. Example of Complete Process

**Objective:** Increase sales
↓
**Problem:** Declining customer demand
↓
**Alternatives:** Reduce price / Improve quality / Increase advertising
↓
**Evaluation:** Compare cost, risk and expected benefits
↓
**Choice:** Improve quality
↓
**Action:** Implement quality improvement
↓
**Results:** Check whether sales have increased

---

## 4. Characteristics of an Effective Decision

An effective decision should have:

* **Action orientation** – should lead to implementation.
* **Goal direction** – should help achieve organizational objectives.
* **Effective implementation** – should consider internal and external factors. 

## Conclusion

Thus, decision-making is a **systematic process of identifying a problem, finding and evaluating alternatives, selecting the best solution, implementing it and examining the results**. It helps managers achieve organizational objectives effectively.
