# Planning Process – 13 Mark Answer

## 1. Introduction

**Planning** is the basic function of management. According to **Koontz and O'Donnell**, planning is an intellectual process involving the conscious determination of courses of action based on objectives and estimates. 

The **planning process** consists of the following steps:

**Perception of Opportunities → Objectives → Planning Premises → Alternatives → Evaluation → Choice → Supporting Plans → Sequence of Activities**

---

## 2. Steps in Planning Process

### 1. Perception of Opportunities

It is the **starting point of planning**. The manager identifies future opportunities and studies the organization's strengths, weaknesses and possible uncertainties.

**Example:** A company identifies increasing demand for electric vehicles. 

### 2. Establishing Objectives

Clear objectives are fixed for the organization and its departments. They specify **what is to be achieved** and provide direction for further planning.

**Example:** To increase sales by **20% within one year**. 

### 3. Considering Planning Premises

Planning premises are **assumptions about future conditions**, such as market demand, prices, costs, production and available resources.

**Example:** A company forecasts future customer demand before increasing production. 

### 4. Identification of Alternatives

Managers identify different possible courses of action for achieving the objectives.

**Example:** To increase sales, a company may consider advertising, reducing prices, entering new markets or launching a new product. 

### 5. Evaluation of Alternatives

Each alternative is evaluated based on **cost, profit, risk, resources and suitability** for achieving the objectives.

**Example:** Increasing advertising may increase sales but also involves higher expenditure. 

### 6. Choice of Alternative Plans

After evaluation, the manager selects the **best alternative** that most effectively achieves the organizational objectives.

**Example:** The company selects digital advertising because it provides better expected results at acceptable cost. 

### 7. Formulating Supporting Plans

Supporting plans are prepared to implement the selected plan. These include **budgets, departmental plans and resource requirements**.

**Example:** Preparing an advertising budget, recruitment plan and production schedule. 

### 8. Establishing Sequence of Activities

The planned activities are arranged in the **proper order** and implemented.

**Example:**
**Market Research → Product Design → Production → Advertising → Distribution → Sales**



---

## 3. Importance of Planning

* Provides clear **direction and objectives**.
* Helps deal with **uncertainty and change**.
* Ensures **economical use of resources**.
* Improves **coordination**.
* Provides standards for **control**.
* Improves **organizational effectiveness**. 

## 4. Conclusion

Planning is a **systematic and continuous process**. By following these steps, managers can select the best course of action, utilize resources effectively and achieve organizational objectives efficiently.
