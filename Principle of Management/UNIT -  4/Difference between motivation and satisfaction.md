# Difference Between Motivation and Satisfaction

### Introduction

**Motivation** and **satisfaction** are closely related concepts in management. Motivation explains the forces that **encourage an employee to act and work toward goals**, whereas satisfaction describes the employee's **level of happiness and contentment with the job**. The given POM notes discuss both concepts under employee behaviour and motivation. POM FULL NOTES

## 1. Meaning of Motivation

The word **motivation** is derived from the Latin word *“movere”*, meaning **“to move.”** Motivation refers to the forces that cause people to behave in certain ways. It includes pressures and influences that **trigger, channel and sustain human behaviour**.

According to **Koontz and O'Donnell**, *“Motivation is a class of drives, needs, wishes and similar forces.”* POM FULL NOTES

Motivation is:
- An internal feeling.
- Related to human needs.
- Goal-oriented.
- Capable of being positive or negative.
- Used by managers to create the **willingness to work** among employees. POM FULL NOTES

## 2. Meaning of Satisfaction

**Employee satisfaction or job satisfaction** refers to whether employees are **happy, contented and able to fulfil their desires and needs at work**.

Employee satisfaction is considered a factor in:
- Employee motivation
- Goal achievement
- Positive employee morale

However, satisfaction alone does not necessarily mean high performance, because even mediocre employees may remain in an organization simply because they are satisfied with the work environment. POM FULL NOTES

## 3. Difference Between Motivation and Satisfaction

| Basis | Motivation | Satisfaction |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | Forces that encourage a person to act or behave in a particular way. | State of being happy and contented with the job. |
| **Nature** | It is an **active process** that drives behaviour. | It is a **state or feeling** resulting from the work experience. |
| **Main focus** | Focuses on **encouraging action and performance**. | Focuses on **happiness and contentment**. |
| **Purpose** | Creates willingness to work toward organizational goals. | Indicates how far employees' needs and desires are fulfilled. |
| **Direction** | Directs behaviour toward a particular goal. | Reflects an employee's attitude toward the job/work environment. |
| **Source** | Can arise from needs, rewards, recognition, incentives or fear. | Can arise from respect, recognition, empowerment, compensation, teamwork and good management. |
| **Effect on employee** | Encourages employees to put in effort and improve performance. | Makes employees feel comfortable, happy and positive about their work. |
| **Measurement** | Observed through willingness, effort, behaviour and goal-directed performance. | Often measured through employee satisfaction surveys and meetings. |
| **Time orientation** | Mainly concerned with **initiating and sustaining behaviour**. | Mainly concerned with the **employee's existing feeling or attitude**. |
| **Example** | A promotion opportunity motivates an employee to work harder. | The employee feels satisfied because the organization provides recognition and a good working environment. |
| **Relationship** | Motivation can contribute to satisfaction. | Satisfaction can support motivation, morale and goal achievement. |

## 4. Factors Contributing to Satisfaction

According to the notes, employee satisfaction can be improved through:

1. **Treating employees with respect**
2. **Regular employee recognition**
3. **Employee empowerment**
4. **Good benefits and compensation**
5. **Employee perks and company activities**
6. **Positive management**
7. **Teamwork**
8. **Effective communication**
9. **Good co-worker interaction**
10. **Understanding the organization's mission and vision** POM FULL NOTES

## 5. Relationship Between Motivation and Satisfaction

Motivation and satisfaction are **not the same**, but they are closely connected.

- Motivation provides the **drive to act**.
- Satisfaction provides the **feeling of contentment** after or during the work experience.
- Proper motivation can improve employee performance and morale.
- A satisfying work environment can support motivation and commitment.
- Therefore, managers should create both **willingness to work through motivation** and a **positive work environment through satisfaction**. The notes specifically state that motivation creates the willingness to work and contributes to productivity and good industrial relations. POM FULL NOTES

### Conclusion

In simple terms, **motivation is the force that makes an employee want to work, while satisfaction is the feeling of happiness and contentment that an employee experiences with the job**. Effective management should focus on both, because motivated and satisfied employees are more likely to contribute positively to organizational goals.

**Easy way to remember:**  
> **Motivation = “I want to work.”**  
> **Satisfaction = “I am happy with my work.”**
