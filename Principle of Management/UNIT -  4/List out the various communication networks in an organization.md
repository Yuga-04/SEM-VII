# Communication Networks in an Organization – 13 Marks

### Introduction

**Communication** is the exchange of messages and information between people to achieve common understanding. In an organization, communication connects employees, helps coordinate activities, and strengthens cooperation among different parts of the organization. POM FULL NOTES(4)

The PDF mainly classifies organizational communication into **formal and informal communication**, with formal communication flowing in three major directions: **downward, upward and horizontal**. POM FULL NOTES(4)

## 1. Formal Communication

Formal communication follows the officially established route in the **organizational structure**. It generally follows the chain of command or line of authority.

There are **three directions** of formal communication:

### A. Downward Communication

Downward communication takes place when a message moves **from a higher level to a lower level** in the organizational hierarchy.

**Examples:**
- Manager giving instructions to employees.
- Management communicating policies.
- Supervisor providing performance feedback.
- Top management communicating objectives to subordinates.

The messages generally contain **directions, instructions, policies and performance feedback**. It follows the formal chain of command and is important for proper organizational functioning. POM FULL NOTES(4)

**Flow:**

**Top Management → Middle Management → Lower Management → Workers**

---

### B. Upward Communication

Upward communication takes place when information moves **from lower levels to higher levels** of the organization.

**Examples:**
- Employees submitting progress reports.
- Workers reporting problems.
- Employees giving suggestions.
- Subordinates communicating successes and failures.
- Employees making complaints or appeals.

It also allows employees to participate in formal organizational decisions and provides feedback to management. POM FULL NOTES(4)

**Flow:**

**Workers → Lower Management → Middle Management → Top Management**

---

### C. Horizontal Communication

Horizontal communication, also called **lateral communication**, takes place between people who are at the **same level of the organizational hierarchy**.

It may occur:
- Between members of the same work group.
- Between different work groups at the same level.
- Between managers at the same level.
- Between employees performing related activities.

It is mainly used for **advice, problem solving and coordination of activities**. POM FULL NOTES(4)

**Flow:**

**Employee ↔ Employee**

or

**Manager ↔ Manager**

---

## 2. Informal Communication – Grapevine

Informal communication does not follow the officially established organizational structure. It generally develops through **interpersonal relationships** among employees.

The most common term for informal communication is **“grapevine.”** It may take the form of gossip or rumours.

### Features of Grapevine

- It spreads information quickly.
- It can cross established organizational boundaries.
- It can connect employees at different organizational levels.
- Information may be changed through **deletion or exaggeration**.
- It may become common when employees feel threatened or when management does not provide sufficient information.
- Therefore, grapevine communication may sometimes result in inaccurate information. POM FULL NOTES(4)

## Communication Network – Simple Diagram

```text
                 TOP MANAGEMENT
                       ↓
              DOWNWARD COMMUNICATION
                       ↓
                MIDDLE MANAGEMENT
                       ↓
              LOWER MANAGEMENT
                       ↓
                    WORKERS

UPWARD COMMUNICATION: ↑

HORIZONTAL COMMUNICATION:
        Employee ↔ Employee
        Manager  ↔ Manager

INFORMAL COMMUNICATION:
        Employee ↔ Employee
          ↕          ↕
        Manager  ↔ Worker
             (Grapevine)
```

## Comparison of Communication Networks

| Type | Direction | Main Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Downward** | Superior → Subordinate | Instructions, policies, feedback |
| **Upward** | Subordinate → Superior | Reports, suggestions, complaints |
| **Horizontal** | Same level ↔ Same level | Coordination, advice, problem solving |
| **Informal/Grapevine** | Any direction | Informal sharing of information |

### Conclusion

An effective organization requires a proper combination of **downward, upward, horizontal and informal communication**. Downward communication provides direction, upward communication provides feedback, horizontal communication promotes coordination, and informal communication enables quick interpersonal information sharing. Thus, communication acts as an important linking mechanism between different members and departments of an organization. POM FULL NOTES(4)
