# Characteristics of Good Communication and Barriers to Communication – 13 Marks

## Introduction

Communication is the **exchange of messages between people for achieving common meaning and understanding**. It links members of an organization and helps them coordinate their activities. Effective communication strengthens cooperation and relationships among employees. POM FULL NOTES(4)

---

# A. Characteristics of Good / Effective Communication

According to the given PDF, effective communication should have the following characteristics:

### 1. Clear Purpose

The sender must first **clarify in his or her mind what has to be communicated**. The purpose of the message and the plan for achieving the intended result should be clear. POM FULL NOTES

### 2. Familiar Symbols and Language

The symbols used for **encoding and decoding** should be familiar to both the sender and receiver. This helps avoid misunderstanding.

### 3. Participation

While planning communication, **other people should be consulted and encouraged to participate**. This improves understanding and acceptance.

### 4. Consider the Receiver's Needs

The sender should consider the **needs of the receiver** and, whenever appropriate, communicate information that is useful and valuable to the receiver.

### 5. Appropriate Tone and Language

The **tone of voice, choice of language and consistency between what is said and how it is said** influence the receiver's reaction. Therefore, the sender should communicate appropriately. POM FULL NOTES

### 6. Proper Feedback

Communication is complete only when the message is **understood by the receiver**. Feedback helps the sender know whether the message has been correctly understood. POM FULL NOTES

### 7. Consideration of Emotions

Communication is not merely the transmission of information. It also involves **emotions**, which are important in relationships between superiors, subordinates and colleagues.

### 8. Responsibility of Both Sender and Receiver

Effective communication is the responsibility of **both the sender and the receiver**. The sender should communicate clearly, while the receiver should properly receive and understand the message. POM FULL NOTES

# B. Barriers to Effective Communication

**Barriers to communication** are factors that **block or significantly distort successful communication**. The important barriers mentioned in the PDF are: POM FULL NOTES

### 1. Filtering

Filtering occurs when the sender **manipulates information** so that it appears more favourable to the receiver.

For example, a manager may hide negative information and communicate only the favourable aspects of a situation. More levels in the organizational hierarchy provide more opportunities for filtering. POM FULL NOTES

### 2. Selective Perception

Selective perception means **seeing or hearing what one wants to see or hear**.

The receiver may interpret a message according to personal needs, background, experience and expectations. Unpleasant information may be ignored or rejected. POM FULL NOTES

### 3. Emotions

The emotional condition of the receiver affects how information is interpreted.

Extreme emotions such as **anger, depression or excessive excitement** can interfere with effective communication. When a person is angry, it becomes difficult to consider another person's viewpoint and choose appropriate words. POM FULL NOTES

### 4. Language

Language becomes a barrier when the message is **unclear, vague or difficult for the receiver to understand**.

Differences in age, education, culture, region and background can cause different interpretations of words. The PDF recommends using simple and direct language and avoiding unnecessary technical jargon. POM FULL NOTES

### 5. Stereotyping

Stereotyping occurs when people have **preconceived ideas about others** and judge them according to a particular category rather than as individuals.

This causes people to hear only information that confirms their existing beliefs, reducing effective personal communication. POM FULL NOTES

### 6. Status Difference

Differences in organizational status can create communication problems, especially between **employees and managers**.

Employees may hesitate to communicate problems honestly to higher-level managers and may distort upward communication. POM FULL NOTES

### 7. Conflicting Signals

Conflicting signals occur when a person sends **inconsistent verbal and non-verbal messages**.

For example, a manager may say, *“My door is always open,”* but appear annoyed whenever an employee approaches. The receiver becomes confused about which message to believe. POM FULL NOTES

### 8. Reluctance to Communicate

Managers or employees may sometimes be reluctant to communicate because:

- They doubt their ability to communicate.
- They dislike writing or speaking.
- They hesitate to communicate bad news because they fear a negative reaction.

Such reluctance becomes a barrier to effective communication. POM FULL NOTES

### 9. Projection

Projection occurs when a person **assumes that others have the same motives or characteristics as themselves**.

For example, a manager motivated by money may assume that employees are also primarily motivated by money. This can create misunderstanding. POM FULL NOTES

### 10. Halo Effect

The halo effect occurs when a person forms an overall opinion based on **one particular characteristic**.

For example, an employee with good attendance may automatically be judged as productive and having a good attitude, even when other aspects of performance have not been properly evaluated. POM FULL NOTES

## Conclusion

Good communication requires **clarity, appropriate language, consideration of the receiver, proper feedback and emotional awareness**. However, barriers such as **filtering, selective perception, emotions, language, stereotyping, status differences, conflicting signals, reluctance, projection and halo effect** can distort the message. Therefore, both sender and receiver must take responsibility for ensuring that communication is clearly understood. POM FULL NOTES
