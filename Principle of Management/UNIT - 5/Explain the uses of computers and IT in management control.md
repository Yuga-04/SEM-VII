# Uses of Computers and IT in Management Control – 13 Marks

## Introduction

**Management control** is the process of ensuring that actual activities conform to planned activities. It involves **measurement of performance, comparison with standards and corrective action**. Computers and Information Technology (IT) make this control process faster, more accurate and easier to manage. POM FULL NOTES(5)

## Uses of Computers and IT in Management Control

### 1. Collection and Storage of Data
Computers help collect and store large amounts of organizational data such as sales, production, expenses, employee performance and inventory details.

### 2. Measurement of Performance
IT systems help managers measure actual performance against established standards. This supports the control process described in the notes. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 3. Comparison of Actual and Planned Performance
Computerized systems can compare actual results with planned or budgeted results and identify variations or deviations.

### 4. Statistical Analysis
Statistical data and statistical analysis are important non-budgetary control techniques. Computers can process large quantities of statistical information and present it clearly to managers. The notes specifically mention **statistical data and its presentation through charts** as a control device. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 5. Preparation of Reports
Computers can generate **special reports and analyses** that help managers understand organizational performance and identify areas requiring attention. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 6. Budgetary Control
Computer-based systems help compare **actual results with budgeted results**. Variances can then be investigated and appropriate remedial action can be taken. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 7. Variance Analysis
IT makes it easier to identify differences between planned and actual performance. Managers can investigate the reasons for these differences and distinguish between controllable and non-controllable factors. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 8. Quick Corrective Action
When deviations or problems are identified, computerized information enables managers to take **remedial or corrective action quickly**. Corrective action is an essential final step in the control process. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 9. Break-Even Analysis
Computers can be used to analyze the relationship between **sales and expenses** and determine the break-even point. Break-even analysis is identified in the notes as an important control device. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 10. Operational Audit
IT systems assist in the analysis of **accounting, financial and other operational information** required for operational or internal audits. The notes identify operational auditing as an effective managerial control tool. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 11. Project Control using PERT
Computers can support **PERT (Program/Project Evaluation and Review Technique)** for analyzing project activities, estimating the time required for tasks and identifying the minimum time needed to complete a project. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 12. Project Scheduling using Gantt Charts
IT tools can prepare **Gantt charts**, which represent project schedules and help managers monitor the progress of different activities. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### 13. Management by Exception
Computerized control systems can highlight significant deviations and bring them to the manager's attention. This saves management time by allowing managers to focus on areas that require corrective action. The notes specifically identify **management by exception** as a benefit of budgetary control. POM FULL NOTES(5)

## Conclusion

Computers and IT make management control **faster, more accurate and systematic**. They help managers collect and analyze data, prepare reports, compare actual performance with plans, identify deviations and take corrective action. Tools such as **statistical analysis, variance analysis, break-even analysis, operational audit, PERT and Gantt charts** strengthen the overall control process.
