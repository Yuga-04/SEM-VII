# Explain the Process of Controlling with a Flow Diagram – 13 Marks

## Introduction

**Control** is the process through which managers ensure that actual activities conform to planned activities. According to **Koontz and O'Donnell**, managerial control involves measuring accomplishment against standards and correcting deviations to ensure the achievement of objectives according to plans. POM FULL NOTES(5)

The main purpose of controlling is to ensure that organizational activities are performed according to the planned standards and that deviations are corrected when necessary.

## 1. Establishment of Standards

The **first step** in the controlling process is establishing standards.

Plans act as the **yardsticks or criteria** against which actual performance is compared. Standards should clearly indicate the expected level of performance.

### Examples of standards:
- **Profitability standards** – Expected profit or return on investment.
- **Market position standards** – Desired market share compared with competitors.
- **Productivity standards** – Expected quantity of production.
- **Product leadership standards** – Standards required to achieve product leadership.
- **Employee attitude standards** – Expected attitudes and behaviour of employees.
- **Social responsibility standards** – Contribution towards society.
- Standards showing the balance between **short-term and long-term goals**. POM FULL NOTES(5)

---

## 2. Measurement of Actual Performance

The second step is to **measure the actual performance** of the organization, department or employee.

Performance should preferably be measured on a **forward-looking basis** so that deviations can be detected in advance and suitable action can be taken.

The difficulty of measurement depends upon the type of activity being measured. Therefore, suitable methods of measurement should be selected for different activities. POM FULL NOTES(5)

**Example:**  
If the production standard is 1,000 units per day, the actual production may be measured as 950 units.

---

## 3. Comparison of Actual Performance with Standards

After measuring actual performance, managers **compare it with the established standards**.

A standard represents the expected level of activity and acts as a model for evaluating organizational performance.

The comparison helps managers determine whether:

- Actual performance **equals the standard** – performance is satisfactory.
- Actual performance is **below the standard** – negative deviation exists.
- Actual performance is **above the standard** – favourable deviation exists.

Thus, comparison helps identify the **deviation between planned and actual performance**. POM FULL NOTES(5)

---

## 4. Taking Corrective Action

If significant deviations are identified, the manager takes **corrective action**.

Corrective action is a managerial activity aimed at bringing actual organizational performance back to the required performance standard.

Before taking corrective action, managers should ensure that:

- The standards were properly established.
- The measurements are valid and reliable.
- The actual cause of the problem has been identified.

Corrective action helps remove mistakes and barriers that prevent the organization from achieving its objectives. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### Examples:
- Providing additional training to employees.
- Improving production methods.
- Allocating additional resources.
- Correcting faulty procedures.
- Revising plans when necessary.

---

## Nature and Purpose of Control

According to the PDF, control has the following characteristics:

1. **Control is an essential function of management.**
2. **Control is an ongoing process.**
3. **Control is forward-working**, because past activities cannot be controlled.
4. **Control involves measurement.**
5. **The essence of control is action.**
6. **Control is an integrated system.** POM FULL NOTES(5)

---

## Conclusion

Controlling is an important managerial function that ensures organizational activities are carried out according to plans. The process begins with **establishing standards**, followed by **measuring actual performance**, **comparing it with the standards**, and finally **taking corrective action** wherever required. Thus, effective control helps the organization achieve its planned objectives efficiently. POM FULL NOTES(5)
