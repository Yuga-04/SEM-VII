# Techniques of Controlling – 13 Marks

**Controlling** is the process of measuring actual performance against standards and taking corrective action when deviations occur. The major techniques of controlling can be classified into **budgetary** and **non-budgetary control techniques**. POM FULL NOTES(5)

## 1. Budgetary Control Techniques

### a) Revenue and Expense Budgets
These budgets specify expected **revenues and operating expenses** in monetary terms. A sales budget is a common revenue budget. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### b) Time, Space, Material and Product Budgets
These budgets express plans in **physical quantities** such as labour-hours, machine-hours, units of materials, space allocated and units produced. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### c) Capital Expenditure Budget
It specifies planned expenditure on **plant, machinery, equipment, inventories and other capital items**. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### d) Cash Budget
It forecasts **cash receipts and cash disbursements** and compares actual cash experience with the forecast.

### e) Variable Budget
A variable budget adjusts costs according to the **level of activity or output**. It considers fixed, semi-variable and variable costs. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### f) Zero-Based Budget
Under zero-based budgeting, each program or activity is treated as a **new budget package**, and its costs are calculated from the ground up rather than simply modifying the previous budget. POM FULL NOTES(5)

# 2. Non-Budgetary Control Techniques

### a) Statistical Data
Statistical analysis of business operations provides information for control. Data may be presented in **tables, charts and other forms** to help managers understand performance. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### b) Special Reports and Analysis
Special reports provide managers with information about organizational activities and help them identify deviations and take suitable action. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### c) Break-Even Point Analysis
A break-even chart shows the relationship between **sales and expenses** and indicates the volume at which revenue exactly equals expenses. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### d) Operational Audit
Operational auditing is the **regular and independent appraisal** of accounting, financial and other business operations by internal auditors. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### e) Personal Observation
Managers can exercise control through **direct personal observation** of activities and performance. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### f) PERT
**Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT)** is used to analyze project activities, estimate the time required for tasks and identify the minimum time required to complete a project. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### g) Gantt Chart
A **Gantt chart** is a bar chart used to illustrate a project schedule, including the start and finish dates of activities. It may also show relationships between activities. POM FULL NOTES(5)

### Conclusion

Thus, controlling techniques provide managers with different methods to **measure performance, identify deviations and take corrective action**. Budgetary techniques focus mainly on planned financial and physical results, while non-budgetary techniques such as **statistical analysis, break-even analysis, operational audit, PERT and Gantt charts** provide additional control mechanisms.
