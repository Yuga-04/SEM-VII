# Different Types of Business Organization – 13 Marks

## 1. Introduction

A **business organization** is a group of people intentionally organized to accomplish a common goal. When starting a new business, one of the important decisions is choosing the **proper structure of the business organization**. The structure determines ownership, responsibility, liability, decision-making and sharing of profits. 

The main types of business organizations given in the PDF are:

1. Sole Proprietorship
2. Partnership
3. Corporation
4. Joint Stock Company
5. Public Corporation

---

## 2. Sole Proprietorship

A **sole proprietorship** is a business owned by **one person**. Usually, the owner is responsible for the day-to-day running of the business. The owner owns the assets and receives the profits, but also has complete personal responsibility for the business's liabilities or debts. Legally, the owner and the business are treated as one. 

### Merits

* It is the **easiest and least expensive** form of ownership to organize.
* The owner has complete control over business decisions.
* The owner receives all income generated by the business.
* Profits flow directly to the owner's personal tax return.
* The business is easy to dissolve when required. 

### Demerits

* It has **unlimited liability**.
* Personal and business assets are at risk.
* It has very limited ability to raise investment funds.
* The owner mainly depends on personal savings or consumer loans.
* It may be difficult to attract highly skilled employees. 

---

## 3. Partnership

A **partnership** is a business in which **two or more people share ownership** of a single business. The partners should have a legal agreement describing how decisions will be made, profits will be shared, disputes will be resolved and how the partnership will be dissolved if necessary. 

### Merits

* Relatively easy to establish.
* More than one owner can increase the ability to raise funds.
* Profits flow directly to the partners' personal taxes.
* Employees may be attracted by the opportunity to become partners. 

### Demerits

* Partners may be jointly and individually liable for the actions of other partners.
* Profits have to be shared.
* Sharing decisions may lead to disagreements.
* Some employee benefits are not deductible from business income.
* The partnership may have a limited life due to withdrawal or death of a partner. 

---

## 4. Corporation

A **corporation** is considered by law as a separate entity from its owners. It can be taxed, sued and can enter into contractual agreements. The owners are called **shareholders**. Shareholders elect a **Board of Directors** to supervise major policies and decisions. The corporation continues to exist even when ownership changes. 

### Merits

* Shareholders have **limited liability** for the corporation's debts.
* Shareholders are generally responsible only for their investment in the company's stock.
* Additional funds can be raised through the sale of stock.
* The corporation may deduct the cost of benefits provided to officers and employees. 

### Demerits

* Incorporation requires more time and money.
* Corporations are subject to government monitoring and regulations.
* More paperwork may be required.
* Incorporation may result in higher overall taxes. 

---

## 5. Joint Stock Company

A **Joint Stock Company** is formed to overcome the limited financial resources and heavy burden of risk found in some other forms of organization. Capital is raised by **selling shares** of different values. The persons who purchase the shares are called **shareholders**.

The **Board of Directors** is the managing body responsible for policy-making and important financial and technical decisions. 

There are two main types:

### a) Private Limited Company

* It can be formed by **two or more persons**.
* Membership is limited to **50** according to the PDF.
* Transfer of shares is limited to members.
* Government interference in its working is limited. 

### b) Public Limited Company

* Membership is open to the general public.
* The minimum number required to form it is **seven**, with no upper limit according to the PDF.
* It can advertise its shares to the general public through a prospectus.
* It is subject to greater control and supervision. 

### Merits

* Limited liability encourages more people to invest.
* Risk of loss is divided among a large number of investors.
* The company is not affected by the death or retirement of shareholders. 

### Disadvantages

* It is difficult to preserve secrecy.
* Many legal formalities have to be followed.
* There may be a lack of personal interest. 

---

## 6. Public Corporation

A **Public Corporation** is wholly owned by the **Government**, either at the central or state level. It is usually established through a **Special Act of Parliament**. The special statute specifies its **management pattern, powers, duties and jurisdiction**. 

---

## 7. Conclusion

Thus, different forms of business organization provide different methods of **ownership, management, raising capital, sharing profits and bearing risk**. The major forms discussed in the PDF are **Sole Proprietorship, Partnership, Corporation, Joint Stock Company and Public Corporation**. Each form has its own merits and demerits, and the appropriate form depends on the nature and requirements of the business.
