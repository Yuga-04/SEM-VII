# Determine an Organization's Exposure to Internal Threats and Evaluate External Security Threats

## 1. Introduction

Security threats are potential dangers that can exploit vulnerabilities in an organization's information systems, resulting in unauthorized access, data loss, or service disruption.

Security threats are classified into two major categories:

- Internal threats: Originate from employees, contractors, or other trusted personnel within an organization.
- External threats: Originate from outside the organization, such as hackers, cybercriminals, and malicious groups.

Organizations must identify these threats, assess their risks, and implement suitable security measures to protect their systems and data.

## 2. Methods to Determine an Organization's Exposure to Internal Threats

Internal threats may be intentional, such as stealing confidential data, or accidental, such as deleting important files or misconfiguring permissions.

The following methods help determine internal threat exposure.

### 1. Identify Critical Assets

Identify important organizational resources, including sensitive data, servers, databases, and network resources. Classify them according to their importance to business operations.

Example: Identifying customer databases and financial records as critical assets.

### 2. Monitor Employee Activities

Monitor employee activities using system logs, audits, and access tracking to identify unusual or unauthorized behaviour.

Example: Detecting an employee copying confidential customer data to an external drive.

### 3. Implement Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

Provide employees access only to the information and resources required for their job roles. This reduces the risk of unauthorized access and misuse.

Example: Allowing only authorized finance employees to access financial records.

### 4. Conduct Internal Vulnerability Assessments

Examine internal systems and processes to identify weaknesses, such as weak passwords, outdated software, misconfigured permissions, and shared credentials.

Example: Identifying an outdated internal application that exposes sensitive information.

### 5. Educate Employees on Security Policies

Provide security awareness training to employees about safe data handling, password protection, phishing, and organizational security policies.

Example: Training employees to recognize suspicious emails and avoid downloading unknown attachments.

### 6. Review and Audit Access Permissions

Regularly examine user accounts and access privileges to ensure that employees have only the permissions necessary for their duties.

Example: Removing system access when an employee leaves the organization or changes roles.

## 3. Methods to Evaluate External Security Threats

External security threats originate outside the organization and attempt to compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems, networks, and data.

The following steps are used to evaluate external security risks.

### 1. Identify Potential External Threats

Identify possible threats such as hackers, malware, ransomware, phishing campaigns, botnets, and Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks. Monitor cybersecurity reports to understand emerging threats.

Example: Identifying phishing emails targeting employees to steal login credentials.

### 2. Assess System Vulnerabilities

Perform vulnerability scans to detect weaknesses in software, hardware, and network configurations. Check for outdated operating systems, unsecured ports, weak authentication, and misconfigured firewalls. Also evaluate third-party vendors and cloud services.

Example: Discovering an unpatched web application that could allow unauthorized database access.

### 3. Determine Likelihood and Potential Impact

Estimate the probability of a threat exploiting a vulnerability and assess its possible consequences.

The potential impacts include:

- Data loss or theft.
- Financial damage.
- Operational disruption.
- Reputational damage.
- Regulatory penalties.

Threats can be classified into low, medium, and high risk based on their likelihood and potential impact.

### 4. Apply Security Countermeasures

Implement appropriate security controls to reduce external threats.

- Firewalls: Filter network traffic and restrict unauthorized access.
- IDS/IPS: Detect suspicious activities and help prevent attacks.
- Antivirus software: Detects and removes malware.
- Encryption: Protects sensitive data during storage and transmission.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA): Adds an extra layer of authentication.
- Network segmentation: Separates critical systems from less secure networks.

Example: Using a firewall, intrusion prevention system, and MFA to protect an organization's online services.

### 5. Review and Update Security Policies Regularly

Continuously monitor network traffic, system logs, and unusual activities. Update security policies, maintain incident response plans, and conduct security awareness training.

Example: Regularly updating firewall rules and software patches to address newly discovered vulnerabilities.

## 4. Difference Between Internal and External Threats

| Basis              | Internal threats                            | External threats                                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Origin             | Within the organization                     | Outside the organization                                  |
| Sources            | Employees, contractors, trusted users       | Hackers, cybercriminals, malicious groups                 |
| Common causes      | Misuse of access, human error, data theft   | Malware, phishing, ransomware, DDoS                       |
| Assessment methods | Activity monitoring, audits, access reviews | Threat identification, vulnerability scans, risk analysis |
| Main controls      | RBAC, access tracking, employee training    | Firewalls, IDS/IPS, encryption, MFA                       |

## Conclusion

Determining internal threat exposure and evaluating external security threats are essential for protecting an organization's information systems. Internal threats can be managed through asset identification, employee monitoring, access control, vulnerability assessments, and security awareness. External threats can be evaluated by identifying potential attacks, assessing vulnerabilities, estimating likelihood and impact, and applying suitable countermeasures.

Continuous monitoring, regular security assessments, and updated security policies help reduce risks and maintain organizational security.
