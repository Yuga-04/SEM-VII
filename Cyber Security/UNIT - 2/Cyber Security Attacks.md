# Cyber Security Attacks – 13 Mark Answer

## 1. Introduction

A **cybersecurity attack** is a deliberate attempt by an attacker to gain unauthorized access to a computer system, network or data, steal information, modify data, or disrupt services. Cyberattacks can affect the **confidentiality, integrity, availability and authenticity** of information.

The given PDF classifies security attacks into **Passive, Active, Insider, Distributed and Physical attacks**. 

---

## 2. Types of Cyber Security Attacks

### 1. Passive Attacks

A **passive attack** occurs when an attacker secretly observes or monitors a system or network to collect information **without changing the data or system operation**.

**Examples:**

* **Eavesdropping (Sniffing):** Intercepting network traffic to capture passwords, credit card details, emails, etc.
* **Traffic Analysis:** Studying communication patterns, timing and frequency to obtain confidential information.

**Impact:**

* Mainly affects **confidentiality**.
* Data and system operations are not modified.
* Difficult to detect because the attacker only observes the communication. 

---

### 2. Active Attacks

An **active attack** involves direct interference with a system. The attacker may modify data, impersonate a legitimate user or disrupt services.

**Examples:**

* **Modification of Data:** Changing data packets while they are being transmitted.
* **Masquerading/Spoofing:** Pretending to be another user or system to obtain unauthorized access.
* **Denial of Service (DoS):** Flooding a server or network with excessive requests so that legitimate users cannot access it.

**Impact:**

* Affects **integrity, availability and authenticity**.
* Usually causes immediate damage or disruption.
* Easier to detect than passive attacks but recovery may be difficult. 

---

### 3. Insider Attacks

An **insider attack** is carried out by a trusted person within an organization, such as an employee or contractor, who misuses their legitimate access.

**Examples:**

* **Data Theft:** Stealing confidential files, trade secrets or personal information.
* **Privilege Misuse:** Using higher-level permissions to bypass security controls.

**Impact:**
Insider attacks are dangerous because the attacker already has authorized access, making detection more difficult. 

---

### 4. Distributed Attacks

In a **distributed attack**, attackers use multiple compromised computers or devices to attack a target simultaneously. These infected systems are commonly controlled as a **botnet**.

**Examples:**

* **Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS):** Sending huge amounts of traffic from many systems to overwhelm the target.
* **Botnet Operations:** Using infected computers to send spam, distribute malware or perform other malicious activities.

**Impact:**

* Large-scale service disruption.
* Loss of reputation.
* Possible financial damage. 

---

### 5. Physical Attacks

A **physical attack** directly targets the hardware or physical infrastructure of an organization.

**Examples:**

* **Theft of Devices:** Stealing laptops, USB drives, servers or storage devices containing sensitive information.
* **Tampering with Equipment:** Modifying routers, cables or other network devices to intercept or alter data.

**Impact:**

* Loss of physical assets.
* Exposure of sensitive information.
* Possible disruption of services. 

---

## 3. Impact of Cybersecurity Attacks

### Impact on Organizations

1. **Financial Loss** – Costs may arise from system recovery, cybersecurity experts, ransom payments and lawsuits.
2. **Reputation Damage** – Data breaches and service outages can reduce customer trust.
3. **Operational Disruption** – Ransomware and DDoS attacks can stop business operations for hours or days.
4. **Legal and Compliance Issues** – Organizations may face penalties and investigations for failing to protect data.
5. **Loss of Intellectual Property** – Trade secrets, source code, designs and business strategies may be stolen. 

### Impact on Individuals

1. **Identity Theft** – Personal information can be used for fraud or creating fake accounts.
2. **Financial Damage** – Bank accounts, credit cards and digital wallets may be compromised.
3. **Loss of Privacy** – Private messages, photographs or medical records may be exposed.
4. **Emotional Stress** – Victims may experience anxiety, fear and helplessness.
5. **Job and Career Risks** – Leakage or alteration of professional information can affect a person's career. 

---

## 4. Simple Classification

| Attack Type     | Main Activity                    | Example                    | Main Impact                |
| --------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Passive**     | Observes information             | Sniffing, Traffic Analysis | Confidentiality            |
| **Active**      | Modifies/disrupts                | DoS, Spoofing              | Integrity, Availability    |
| **Insider**     | Misuses authorized access        | Data theft                 | Confidentiality, Integrity |
| **Distributed** | Multiple systems attack together | DDoS, Botnet               | Availability               |
| **Physical**    | Attacks hardware                 | Device theft, Tampering    | Assets & Data              |

---

## 5. Conclusion

Cybersecurity attacks can originate from **outside attackers, insiders, compromised systems or physical threats**. Passive attacks mainly steal information through observation, while active attacks directly modify or disrupt systems. Insider, distributed and physical attacks create additional risks for organizations and individuals. Therefore, organizations must implement strong security controls to protect their **data, systems, users and services**.
