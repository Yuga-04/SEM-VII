# 29(a) Explain the various types of cybercrimes like Identity Theft, Cyberstalking and Investment Fraud with suitable real-world examples.

## 1. Introduction

Cybercrime refers to criminal activities carried out using computers, networks or the internet. Cybercriminals may steal personal information, harass people online or cheat victims through fake financial schemes. Identity theft, cyberstalking and investment fraud are common types of cybercrime.&#x20;

UNIT 5.docx



## 2. Identity Theft

Definition: Identity theft occurs when an attacker steals personal information, such as bank details, login credentials or identification information, to commit fraud.

### Methods

- Phishing emails and fake websites.
- Stealing information from data breaches.
- Using skimming devices on ATMs or card readers.
- Social engineering through phone calls or social media.

### Impacts

- Unauthorized bank transactions.
- Financial loss and damage to financial reputation.
- Legal complications and fraud investigations.

### Real-world example

A person receives a fake bank message asking them to update their account. After entering their banking credentials on the fake website, a criminal uses the stolen information to make unauthorized transactions.

### Prevention

- Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication.
- Monitor bank accounts regularly.
- Avoid sharing sensitive personal information with unknown people.
- Secure personal documents and accounts.

## 3. Cyberstalking

Definition: Cyberstalking is the repeated use of the internet, email or social media to harass, intimidate or threaten another person.

### Methods

- Sending threatening or abusive messages.
- Tracking a victim's online activity or location.
- Creating fake social media accounts to impersonate someone.
- Using spyware or malware to monitor activities.

### Impacts

- Fear, stress and emotional trauma.
- Loss of privacy.
- Possible physical safety risks.
- Disruption of social and professional life.

### Real-world example

A person repeatedly creates fake social media accounts to send threatening messages to another person and monitors their online activity to intimidate them.

### Prevention

- Strengthen social media privacy settings.
- Avoid responding to harassing messages.
- Report incidents to the platform and appropriate authorities.
- Keep screenshots and records as evidence.
- Use security software to detect spyware.

## 4. Investment Fraud

Definition: Investment fraud occurs when criminals use online platforms to trick people into investing money in fake schemes, products or cryptocurrencies.

### Methods

- Fake investment websites.
- Ponzi schemes.
- Social media messages promising unusually high returns.
- Impersonation of financial advisors or institutions.

### Impacts

- Financial loss and depletion of savings.
- Emotional stress.
- Loss of trust in genuine investment opportunities.

### Real-world example

A fraudster advertises an online investment scheme promising guaranteed high returns. A victim transfers money to the platform, but the website disappears and the money cannot be recovered.

### Prevention

- Verify investment platforms and financial advisors.
- Avoid offers promising unusually high or guaranteed returns.
- Use official channels for transactions.
- Report suspicious schemes to the appropriate authorities, such as SEBI in India.

## 5. Conclusion

Identity theft, cyberstalking and investment fraud affect personal privacy, safety and financial security. Strong authentication, careful online behavior, awareness and timely reporting help prevent these cybercrimes.

# 29(b) Write a detailed note on Digital Forensics Tools and their applications in cybercrime investigation.

## 1. Introduction

Digital forensics is the process of identifying, collecting, preserving, analyzing and presenting digital evidence in a legally acceptable manner. It helps investigate cybercrimes such as hacking, financial fraud, identity theft and cyberstalking.&#x20;

UNIT 5.docx



## 2. Objectives of digital forensics

- Identify evidence related to cybercrime.
- Preserve the integrity of digital evidence.
- Determine the source and impact of an attack.
- Recover deleted or damaged information.
- Support legal proceedings with documented evidence.

## 3. Digital forensics tools and applications

### 1. EnCase

Application:

- Creates forensic images of storage devices.
- Recovers and analyzes digital data.
- Generates reports for investigations.

Example: Investigating files on a computer suspected of being used for unauthorized activities.

### 2. FTK (Forensic Toolkit)

Application:

- Examines files and storage devices.
- Supports email examination.
- Helps recover and analyze digital information.

Example: Examining emails and files during a suspected financial fraud investigation.

### 3. Autopsy / Sleuth Kit

Application:

- Performs disk and file-system analysis.
- Helps recover deleted files.
- Creates timelines of computer activity.
- Generates investigation reports.

Example: Recovering deleted files that may contain evidence of data theft.

### 4. Wireshark

Application:

- Captures network packets.
- Analyzes network traffic.
- Helps identify suspicious communication and connections.

Example: Investigating unusual network traffic during a suspected cyberattack.

### 5. Volatility

Application:

- Performs memory forensics.
- Analyzes RAM contents.
- Helps identify running processes and traces of malicious activity.

Example: Examining memory from a compromised computer to investigate possible malware.

### 6. X-Ways Forensics

Application:

- Examines disks and file systems.
- Supports advanced forensic analysis.
- Helps investigate digital evidence.

### 7. Cellebrite UFED

Application:

- Extracts and examines data from smartphones and mobile devices.
- Supports mobile device investigations.

Example: Analyzing relevant mobile-device evidence in an authorized cybercrime investigation.

### 8. Magnet AXIOM

Application:

- Collects and analyzes mobile and cloud-related evidence.
- Helps organize digital artifacts for investigation.

### 9. Tcpdump

Application:

- Captures network packets using a command-line interface.
- Helps investigate suspicious network communication.

These tools and their applications are covered in the Unit 5 material.&#x20;

## 4. Digital forensic investigation process

1\. Identification

Identify devices, accounts and possible sources of evidence.

2\. Preservation

Protect evidence and document the chain of custody.

3\. Collection

Collect data and create forensic copies of storage devices.

4\. Examination and Analysis

Recover relevant data and reconstruct events.

5\. Reporting and Presentation

Document findings and present evidence to authorized stakeholders or a court.

The investigation process must maintain evidence integrity and proper documentation.&#x20;

## 5. Important concepts

- Digital evidence: Information stored or transmitted digitally that may support an investigation.
- Chain of custody: A record of who collected, handled and analyzed the evidence.
- Forensic imaging: Creating a bit-by-bit copy of storage media for examination without altering the original.
- Volatile data: Information, such as RAM contents, that may disappear when a system is powered off.
- Non-volatile data: Information stored persistently on disks or other storage media.

## 6. Conclusion

Digital forensics tools help investigators recover data, analyze network traffic, examine memory and identify evidence of cybercrime. Proper evidence preservation and documentation make investigation results more reliable and suitable for legal proceedings.

# 30(a) Discuss various techniques for Preventing Infection and Finding Infected Hosts in a network.

## 1. Introduction

Malware, ransomware and other malicious software can infect computers and spread across networks. Organizations must prevent malware from entering systems and identify infected hosts quickly to reduce data loss, downtime and financial damage.&#x20;

UNIT 5.docx



## 2. Techniques for preventing infection

### 1. Endpoint protection

- Install antivirus and anti-malware software on all devices.
- Enable real-time scanning.
- Update malware definitions automatically.
- Detect and remove malicious files.

Example: Windows Security detects and quarantines a malicious file.

### 2. Network security measures

- Deploy firewalls and Next-Generation Firewalls (NGFWs).
- Use IDS/IPS to monitor suspicious traffic and block attacks.
- Separate critical systems using network segmentation.
- Restrict unauthorized network connections.

Example: An IPS blocks malicious network traffic before it reaches a protected server.

### 3. Patch management

- Update operating systems and applications regularly.
- Install critical security patches promptly.
- Update firmware on network devices.
- Remove known vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them.

### 4. Safe user practices

- Avoid suspicious links and email attachments.
- Use strong passwords and multi-factor authentication.
- Restrict administrative privileges.
- Train users to identify phishing attacks.

### 5. Email and web security

- Use email filters to block spam and phishing messages.
- Apply web filtering to block malicious websites.
- Restrict downloads from untrusted sources.

### 6. Backup and recovery

- Maintain regular backups of important data.
- Store protected copies separately from normal systems.
- Test recovery procedures.
- Restore clean data if a system becomes infected.

These preventive measures follow the Unit 5 notes.&#x20;

## 3. Techniques for finding infected hosts

### 1. Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)

EDR monitors endpoint activities and identifies suspicious processes or behavior. It can alert administrators and isolate infected devices.

Example: EDR detects ransomware-like activity on a laptop and isolates it from the network.

### 2. Network monitoring

- Analyze network traffic for unusual communication.
- Identify connections to suspicious IP addresses or domains.
- Monitor abnormal traffic patterns.
- Use NetFlow and anomaly detection tools.

Example: Monitoring detects a computer repeatedly communicating with a known malicious server.

### 3. Log analysis

Examine system, firewall and application logs for:

- Repeated failed login attempts.
- Unusual file modifications.
- Unauthorized access.
- Suspicious processes or connections.

### 4. Malware scanning

- Run updated antivirus scans.
- Scan removable storage devices.
- Check network drives.
- Quarantine or remove detected malware.

### 5. Threat intelligence integration

Compare network activity, IP addresses, domains and files against known threat intelligence databases to identify indicators of compromise.

### 6. Incident response

- Isolate suspected infected hosts.
- Investigate the source of infection.
- Identify other affected devices.
- Remove malware and close the exploited vulnerability.
- Restore systems using clean backups.
- Monitor systems for reinfection.

- Reduces the spread of malware.
- Minimizes data loss and downtime.
- Improves early detection of attacks.
- Supports faster recovery.

## 5. Conclusion

Preventing infection requires endpoint protection, firewalls, patching, safe user practices and backups. Finding infected hosts requires EDR, network monitoring, log analysis, malware scanning and incident response. Using both approaches helps organizations maintain a secure network.

# 30(b) Discuss threat detection and mitigation strategies using IDS, IPS and NGFW together in a security framework.

## 1. Introduction

An Intrusion Detection System (IDS), Intrusion Prevention System (IPS) and Next-Generation Firewall (NGFW) are important network security technologies.

- IDS detects suspicious activity and alerts administrators.
- IPS detects and actively blocks malicious traffic.
- NGFW combines traditional firewall filtering with advanced inspection and threat-prevention features.

When used together, they provide layered security and help detect, prevent and mitigate cyberattacks.&#x20;

UNIT 5.docx



## 2. Intrusion Detection System (IDS)

IDS monitors network traffic or system activity to identify possible attacks.

### Functions

- Monitors network packets and system events.
- Detects known attack signatures.
- Identifies unusual behavior.
- Generates alerts and logs.
- Supports investigation and forensic analysis.

### Types

1. Network-based IDS (NIDS): Monitors traffic across a network.
2. Host-based IDS (HIDS): Monitors activities on an individual computer or server.
3. Signature-based IDS: Detects known attack patterns.
4. Anomaly-based IDS: Detects deviations from normal behavior.

Example: Snort can detect suspicious network traffic and alert the security administrator.

## 3. Intrusion Prevention System (IPS)

IPS monitors traffic and takes action to prevent malicious activity.

### Functions

- Inspects traffic in real time.
- Detects malicious packets.
- Drops or blocks suspicious traffic.
- Can reset connections.
- May trigger firewall changes or quarantine compromised hosts.

Example: An IPS blocks traffic matching a known exploit pattern before it reaches a server.

## 4. Next-Generation Firewall (NGFW)

An NGFW is an advanced firewall that combines conventional traffic filtering with additional security capabilities.

### Features

1. Application awareness: Identifies and controls applications.
2. Integrated IPS: Detects and blocks attacks.
3. Deep Packet Inspection (DPI): Inspects packet contents for malicious activity.
4. User identity awareness: Applies policies based on user identity.
5. Threat intelligence: Uses threat databases to identify malicious indicators.
6. SSL/TLS inspection: Can inspect encrypted traffic under appropriately configured policies.
7. Security integration: Shares information with SIEM and endpoint security platforms.

Examples: Cisco Firepower, Fortinet FortiGate, Palo Alto Networks NGFW and Check Point NGFW.&#x20;

UNIT 5.docx



## 5. Combined security framework

Internet / External Traffic

NGFW

Filters traffic, controls applications and performs deep packet inspection.

IPS

Blocks or drops malicious traffic in real time.

Protected Network and Servers

IDS + Centralized Monitoring / SIEM

Detects suspicious activity, generates alerts and records events for investigation.

Note: This is a conceptual layout. In real networks, IDS sensors may monitor traffic at multiple points, while IPS is usually placed inline. NGFW products may already include IPS functionality.

## 6. How they work together

1. Traffic filtering: NGFW checks incoming and outgoing traffic against security policies.
2. Deep inspection: NGFW examines applications and packet contents to identify suspicious activity.
3. Threat detection: IDS monitors traffic and identifies known attacks or unusual behavior.
4. Attack prevention: IPS blocks malicious packets or resets suspicious connections.
5. Alerting and logging: Security events are recorded and sent to monitoring systems.
6. Incident response: Administrators investigate alerts, isolate affected hosts and apply corrective measures.
7. Continuous improvement: Security rules and threat intelligence are updated as new threats emerge.

## 7. Example scenario

Suppose an attacker attempts to exploit a vulnerable web server using malicious HTTP requests.

- NGFW: Inspects the requests and applies web traffic security rules.
- IDS: Detects suspicious requests and alerts the security team.
- IPS: Blocks the malicious requests if they match its prevention rules.
- SIEM: Collects and correlates alerts and logs for investigation.

This layered approach reduces the chance that a single missed detection will lead to a successful attack.

## 8. Advantages

- Provides multiple layers of protection.
- Improves visibility into network threats.
- Helps detect known and unusual attacks.
- Enables real-time blocking of malicious traffic.
- Supports investigation and incident response.
- Reduces the risk of unauthorized access and service disruption.

## 9. Conclusion

IDS, IPS and NGFW perform complementary functions. IDS detects and alerts, IPS actively blocks threats, and NGFW provides advanced traffic filtering and inspection. Their integration improves threat detection, mitigation and overall network security.

# 28(b) Compare and contrast Intrusion Detection System (IDS) and Intrusion Prevention System (IPS) with suitable examples.

## 1. Introduction

IDS and IPS are security technologies used to monitor network traffic and identify malicious activity. The main difference is that IDS detects and reports threats, while IPS can actively block them.&#x20;

UNIT 5.docx



## 2. Intrusion Detection System (IDS)

IDS monitors network traffic or host activities to identify suspicious behavior and generates alerts for administrators.

### Functions

- Monitors network traffic.
- Detects known attacks using signatures.
- Identifies unusual behavior using anomaly detection.
- Generates alerts and maintains logs.
- Helps security teams investigate incidents.

Example: Snort configured as an IDS detects suspicious packets and sends an alert without automatically blocking the traffic.

## 3. Intrusion Prevention System (IPS)

IPS monitors traffic and actively takes action when it identifies malicious activity.

### Functions

- Inspects network traffic continuously.
- Detects suspicious or malicious packets.
- Blocks or drops malicious packets.
- Resets suspicious connections.
- May quarantine compromised hosts or trigger firewall changes.

Example: An IPS blocks network traffic matching a known attack signature before it reaches a protected server.

## 4. Differences between IDS and IPS

| Feature                      | IDS                                         | IPS                                           |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Full form                    | Intrusion Detection System                  | Intrusion Prevention System                   |
| Main purpose                 | Detects attacks                             | Detects and prevents attacks                  |
| Operation                    | Commonly monitors traffic passively         | Commonly operates inline                      |
| Response                     | Sends alerts                                | Blocks or drops malicious traffic             |
| Traffic handling             | Usually does not block traffic directly     | Can stop malicious traffic in real time       |
| Effect on legitimate traffic | Does not usually interrupt traffic directly | Misconfiguration may block legitimate traffic |
| Main benefit                 | Visibility and investigation                | Active prevention                             |
| Example                      | Snort in IDS mode                           | Snort in IPS mode or an inline IPS appliance  |

## 5. Types

### Types of IDS

1. NIDS: Monitors network traffic.
2. HIDS: Monitors activities on individual hosts.
3. Signature-based IDS: Detects known attack patterns.
4. Anomaly-based IDS: Detects deviations from normal activity.

### IPS operation

An IPS may inspect network traffic inline, identify malicious activity using signatures or behavioral analysis, and block the suspicious traffic before it reaches its destination.

## 6. Working process

IDS:

1. Collects packets and logs.
2. Analyzes them using signatures or behavioral baselines.
3. Detects suspicious activity.
4. Generates an alert.
5. Records information for investigation.

IPS:

1. Inspects traffic inline.
2. Analyzes packets and identifies threats.
3. Blocks or drops malicious packets.
4. May reset the connection.
5. Records the event for review.

## 7. Advantages and limitations

IDS advantages

- Provides visibility into suspicious activities.
- Supports forensic investigation.
- Helps identify attacks without directly blocking normal traffic.

IDS limitations

- Requires administrators to respond to alerts.
- May generate false positives.
- May miss unknown attacks.

IPS advantages

- Can block attacks in real time.
- Reduces the time between detection and response.
- Helps prevent malicious traffic from reaching protected systems.

IPS limitations

- Incorrect rules may block legitimate traffic.
- Requires careful configuration and maintenance.
- Deep packet inspection can consume system resources.

## 8. Conclusion

IDS and IPS are complementary security technologies. IDS detects suspicious activity and alerts administrators, whereas IPS detects and blocks malicious traffic. Using both appropriately improves an organization's ability to identify, investigate and prevent cyberattacks.
