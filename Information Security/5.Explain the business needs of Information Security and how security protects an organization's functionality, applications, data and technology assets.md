# 5. Business Needs of Information Security

## Introduction

The purpose of **Information Security Management** is to ensure **business continuity** and reduce business damage by preventing and minimizing the impact of security incidents. It ensures that information remains **confidential, accurate and available** when needed. 

Information security performs **four important functions** for an organization:

1. Protects the organization's ability to function.
2. Enables the safe operation of applications.
3. Protects the data collected and used by the organization.
4. Safeguards technology assets. 

---

## 1. Protecting the Organization's Ability to Function

Information security protects the organization's normal operations from security incidents and business interruptions.

* **General management, IT management and information security management** share responsibility for protecting organizational functionality.
* Security should be treated as a **business and risk-management issue**, not only as a technical problem.
* Management must consider the **business impact and cost of interruption** while making security decisions.
* Proper policies and their enforcement help maintain continuous operations. 

**Example:** Protecting a company's network from attacks prevents employees from losing access to important business services.

---

## 2. Enabling the Safe Operation of Applications

Modern organizations depend on integrated and efficient applications for their daily activities.

Information security provides a safe environment for:

* Operating systems
* Business applications
* E-mail systems
* Instant messaging applications

These applications may be developed internally or obtained from service providers. Management must continuously oversee their security instead of leaving the entire responsibility to the IT department. 

**Example:** Application security prevents unauthorized users from accessing or modifying a company's business application.

---

## 3. Protecting Organizational Data

Data is essential because organizations depend on it for **transactions, services and business operations**. Loss or corruption of data can reduce an organization's ability to provide value to customers. 

Data must be protected:

* **In transmission** – while data is being transferred.
* **In processing** – while data is being used.
* **At rest** – while data is stored.

Database security maintains the **confidentiality, integrity and availability (CIA)** of important data. Controls include:

* Access control
* Authentication
* Auditing
* Encryption
* Backup and recovery
* Integrity controls
* Physical security 

---

## 4. Safeguarding Technology Assets

Organizations depend on secure **hardware and infrastructure** to perform effectively.

As an organization grows, outdated security technologies should be replaced with more robust solutions. Examples include:

* Firewalls
* Intrusion Detection and Prevention Systems (IDS/IPS)
* Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)
* Virtual Private Networks (VPN)

The growth of **Internet, Web and cloud-based services** provides new business opportunities but also introduces additional security risks. 

---

## Conclusion

Information security is a **business requirement, not merely a technical requirement**. It helps an organization continue its operations, safely use applications, protect valuable data and safeguard technology assets. Effective security therefore reduces business interruption, protects organizational resources and supports **confidentiality, integrity and availability** of information.
