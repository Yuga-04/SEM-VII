# 2. Describe the Different Types of Laws and Ethics in Information Security

## Introduction

**Laws and ethics** are important in information security because they define acceptable and unacceptable behavior while using information systems. Laws are rules established by society or government, whereas ethics are principles that guide people to behave responsibly and correctly. 

## Types of Laws in Information Security

### 1. Civil Law

Civil law deals with **non-criminal disputes** between individuals, organizations or entities. It applies when someone suffers loss, harm or damage because of another party's actions involving information systems or data. 

### 2. Criminal Law

Criminal law deals with actions involving computers, networks or data that are considered **illegal and punishable by the government**. Punishments may include fines, probation or imprisonment. 

### 3. Tort Law

Tort law deals with **wrongful acts that cause harm, loss or injury** to another person or organization, even when there is no contract or criminal offense. The affected party may sue for damages or ask the court to stop the harmful activity. 

### 4. Private Law

Private law governs **relationships and disputes between private individuals or organizations**. It focuses on their rights, duties and obligations. 

### 5. Public Law

Public law governs the relationship between **individuals/organizations and the government**. It includes areas such as national security, cybercrime enforcement and government regulation of data and technology.

## Important Information Security Laws

The PDF lists several important laws, including:

* **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (1986)** – addresses computer-related threats and abuse.
* **Computer Security Act (1987)** – deals with federal agency information security.
* **Electronic Communications Privacy Act (1986)** – provides protection for electronic communications.
* **Federal Privacy Act (1974)** – governs the use of personal information by federal agencies.
* **Economic Espionage Act (1996)** – protects trade secrets.
* **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) (1996)** – protects sensitive health information.
* **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (2002)** – relates to financial reporting and corporate accountability.
* **USA PATRIOT Act (2001)** – includes provisions related to terrorism and security.

# Ethics in Information Security

**Ethics** refers to principles of right and wrong behavior. Ethical behavior is especially important because information-security professionals handle sensitive data and powerful technologies.

### 1. Cultural Differences in Ethical Concepts

Different cultures may have different ideas about what is **ethical or unethical**. Therefore, ethical sensitivity to computer use can vary between nationalities and cultures. Conflicts may occur when the ethical behavior of one nationality differs from another. 

### 2. Ethics and Education

Employees should be **properly trained and made aware of information-security issues**. Training helps employees understand expected security behavior and reduces improper use of information systems. 

### 3. Policies and Ethical Behavior

Organizations develop policies that establish expected behavior. Employees should understand and follow these policies when handling organizational information and systems.

---

## Conclusion

Laws provide the **legal framework** for protecting information, while ethics guide individuals to use information and technology **responsibly**. The major types of laws are **civil, criminal, tort, private and public law**. Following laws, organizational policies and ethical principles helps protect information systems and maintain trust and security.
