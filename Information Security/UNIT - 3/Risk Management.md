### Risk Calculation

The material gives:

**Risk = Likelihood × Asset Value − Risk already controlled + Uncertainty**

For example:

```text
Asset Value = 50
Likelihood = 1.0
Existing Control = 0%
Uncertainty = 10%

Risk = (1.0 × 50) − 0% + 10%
```

## 3. Risk Control

Risk control is the process of **applying controls to reduce risks to organizational data and information systems**. Information_Security_Unit_III (…

There are **four main strategies**:

### 1. Risk Avoidance

It attempts to **prevent the exploitation of vulnerabilities**.

Methods include:

- Security policies
- Training and education
- Security technologies
- Removing vulnerabilities
- Limiting access

**Example:** Disabling an unnecessary service to remove its vulnerability. Information_Security_Unit_III (…

### 2. Risk Transference

It means **shifting the risk to another organization, process or asset**.

Examples:

- Insurance
- Outsourcing
- Service contracts

### 3. Risk Mitigation

Mitigation reduces the **impact of an attack** through planning and preparation.

It includes:

- **IRP – Incident Response Plan:** Actions during an incident.
- **DRP – Disaster Recovery Plan:** Recovery after a disaster.
- **BCP – Business Continuity Plan:** Continuation of business during major disasters. Information_Security_Unit_III (…

### 4. Risk Acceptance

It means **accepting the risk without additional protection** when the cost of protection is greater than the possible benefit.

Before accepting the risk, the organization considers the risk level, probability of attack, possible damage and cost-benefit analysis. Information_Security_Unit_III (…

## Evaluation and Maintenance

After implementing controls, they must be **continuously monitored, measured and evaluated**.

This helps determine whether:

- Security controls are effective.
- Residual risk is acceptable.
- Changes are required.

Therefore, **risk management is a continuous process**. Information_Security_Unit_III (…

## Conclusion

Risk management helps an organization protect its information assets by identifying **assets, threats and vulnerabilities**, assessing the level of risk, and selecting suitable controls.

The four important risk control strategies are:

**Avoidance → Transference → Mitigation → Acceptance**
