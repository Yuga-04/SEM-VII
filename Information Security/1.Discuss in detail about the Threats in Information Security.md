# Threats in Information Security – 13 Mark Answer

## Introduction

An **information security threat** is a potential danger that can cause loss or damage to an information asset. An **attack** is an actual action against the asset, while a **vulnerability** is a weakness that can be exploited. 

The major threats to information security are:

### 1. Compromises to Intellectual Property

Intellectual Property (IP) includes **trade secrets, copyrights, trademarks and patents**. Unauthorized use or duplication of IP is a threat. A common example is **software piracy**, where software is illegally copied or distributed. 

### 2. Deviations in Quality of Service

This occurs when a product or service is not delivered as expected. Information systems depend on **power grids, telecom networks, suppliers, service providers and ISPs**. Service failures can reduce information availability. Power shortages, losses and voltage surges can also damage equipment.  

### 3. Deliberate Acts of Espionage or Trespass

These involve **unauthorized access to confidential information**. Common methods include:

* Competitive intelligence
* Industrial espionage
* Shoulder surfing
* Hacking

Proper **authentication and authorization** help protect systems from unauthorized access. 

### 4. Forces of Nature

Natural events can damage information systems and equipment. Examples include **fire, flood, earthquake, lightning, landslide, tornado, hurricane, tsunami, ESD and dust contamination**. Organizations should use contingency and disaster-recovery plans to reduce their impact.

### 5. Acts of Human Error or Failure

Security problems may occur due to mistakes by authorized users, such as:

* Incorrect data entry
* Accidental deletion or modification of data
* Improper data storage
* Failure to protect information

**Training, awareness and verification procedures** can reduce these threats.

### 6. Deliberate Acts of Information Extortion

An attacker or trusted insider may **steal information and demand compensation** for returning it or for not disclosing it. 

### 7. Deliberate Software Attacks

These attacks use **malicious software or malware** to damage, destroy or deny services. Major examples are:

* **Virus** – replicates by attaching to programs or documents.
* **Worm** – independently replicates and spreads through networks.
* **Trojan horse** – disguises its true nature and may damage or steal information.
* **Backdoor/Trapdoor** – provides unauthorized access to a system.
* **Polymorphic malware** – changes its appearance to avoid detection. 

### 8. Deliberate Acts of Sabotage or Vandalism

These acts aim to **destroy assets or damage an organization's image**. **Cyber terrorism** is an example where attackers use networks or the Internet for terrorist activities. 

### 9. Technical Hardware Failures or Errors

Hardware failures can cause **unreliable services, loss of availability and equipment damage**, affecting normal business operations. 

### 10. Technical Software Failures or Errors

Errors in software can cause systems to **malfunction, produce incorrect results or become unavailable**. 

### 11. Technological Obsolescence

Outdated technology can make systems **unreliable and untrustworthy** and increase the risk of loss of data integrity. 

### 12. Deliberate Acts of Theft

Theft is the illegal taking of organizational property. Property may be:

* Physical
* Electronic
* Intellectual

Physical theft can be controlled using **alarm systems and trained security professionals**. 

## Conclusion

Information security threats can arise from **people, technology, malicious software, service failures and natural events**. Organizations must identify these threats, protect vulnerabilities and implement suitable security controls to maintain the **confidentiality, integrity and availability** of information.
